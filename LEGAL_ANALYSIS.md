# Legal Audit & Disciplinary Rationale

## Choice of License: GNU General Public License v3.0 (GPL-3.0)
The selection of the GPL-3.0 license is a strategic decision tailored for a public-sector project like the BOSC Community Library. Unlike permissive licenses such as MIT, the GPL-3.0 is a "copyleft" license, meaning any derivative works must also be released under the same open-source terms. For a government-facing project, this ensures permanent transparency and prevents taxpayer-funded software from being enclosed in proprietary, closed-source versions by third-party vendors.

## Patent Grants and Trademark Protections
A significant advantage of the GPLv3 over other options is its explicit patent grant. It protects the Ministry of Education and other users by stating that any contributor to the library also grants a royalty-free patent license for their contribution. This prevents "patent trolling," where an entity contributes code and later attempts to sue users for patent infringement—a legal safety net essential for wide-scale public adoption and trust in state systems.

## Implications for Commercial Entities
While commercial entities can build services around the BOSC library, they cannot create a "paid, closed-source" version of the library itself. If a company modifies the library for a specific government branch and distributes it, they are legally obligated to share those modifications. This encourages a "Service-Based Model" (similar to Red Hat) where the software remains free, but companies compete to provide the best technical support, security auditing, and implementation services.
